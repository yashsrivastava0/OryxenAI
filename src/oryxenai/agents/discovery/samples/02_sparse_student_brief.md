# Portfolio Discovery Brief — State University Student (working identity)

## Portfolio direction at a glance

**Primary goal:** A modest, honest student portfolio that helps the user secure a software engineering internship next summer.

**Primary professional identity:** Third-year computer science student with strong coursework fundamentals and two small personal projects, leaning toward backend work.

**Primary audience:** Summer-internship recruiters and hiring managers who review many student applications quickly; the portfolio should be skimmable and truthful.

**Desired visitor action:** Contact the student about an internship opportunity.

**Recommended leading emphasis:** Coursework and fundamentals first, with the two projects presented as genuine learning work — not inflated into major engineering feats.

**Current confidence:** Direction and positioning are clear. There is no work experience, no metrics, and no public link yet; the brief keeps every claim inside what the user actually built.

## User intent and definition of success

The user wants an internship for next summer and needs something to show for it. Success means a recruiter can see, in under two minutes: a student who understands core computer science concepts, is actively building small things, prefers backend work, and communicates honestly. The portfolio does not need to compete with senior portfolios — it needs to be credible at the student level and leave a contactable impression.

## Professional identity and positioning inputs

- Fundamentals: data structures, algorithms, operating systems, databases.
- Hands-on curiosity: built working projects outside required assignments.
- Clear preference for backend and data-oriented work.

**Secondary strengths:**

- Web fundamentals (HTML/JS from the weather page).
- Relational basics (SQLite terminal app).

**Positioning caution:** Do not present as an experienced engineer. "Student," "coursework," and "learning projects" are features here, not weaknesses — internships expect them.
